HYPERTENSION IN ADULTS: PRIMARY CARE PATHWAY

Synthetic sample document written for the CiteRx demo. For illustration only; not clinical guidance.

1 Diagnosis

Hypertension is confirmed when clinic blood pressure is 140/90 mmHg or higher and ambulatory or home monitoring gives an average of 135/85 mmHg or higher.
Offer ambulatory blood pressure monitoring to confirm the diagnosis whenever clinic readings are raised.
Clinic blood pressure of 180/120 mmHg or higher is severe hypertension and needs same-day assessment for target organ damage.

2 Treatment Targets

For adults under 80 years, aim for a clinic blood pressure below 140/90 mmHg.
For adults aged 80 and over, aim for a clinic blood pressure below 150/90 mmHg.
In people with diabetes or chronic kidney disease with proteinuria, a target below 130/80 mmHg is commonly used.

3 Lifestyle Measures

Advise reducing salt intake to less than 6 g per day, regular aerobic exercise, weight loss if overweight, limiting alcohol, and stopping smoking.
Lifestyle advice should continue alongside medication.

4 First-Line Medication

For adults under 55 without African or African-Caribbean family origin, and for anyone with diabetes, start an ACE inhibitor or an angiotensin II receptor blocker (ARB).
For adults aged 55 and over, or of African or African-Caribbean family origin without diabetes, start a calcium channel blocker such as amlodipine.
Typical starting doses are ramipril 2.5 mg once daily or amlodipine 5 mg once daily.

WARNING: ACE inhibitors and ARBs are contraindicated in pregnancy and should be stopped as soon as pregnancy is confirmed.
Do not combine an ACE inhibitor with an ARB, because the combination increases the risk of hyperkalaemia and acute kidney injury.

Check serum potassium and eGFR before starting an ACE inhibitor or ARB and again 1 to 2 weeks after starting or after each dose increase.
Stop the ACE inhibitor if the patient develops angioedema; do not re-challenge.

5 Step-Up Treatment

If blood pressure is not controlled on one drug, add a second drug from a different class: an ACE inhibitor or ARB plus a calcium channel blocker or a thiazide-like diuretic such as indapamide.
If three drugs fail to control blood pressure, this is resistant hypertension; check adherence and consider adding low-dose spironolactone if potassium is 4.5 mmol/L or lower.

6 Monitoring

Review patients monthly after starting or changing treatment until blood pressure is at target, then at least once a year.
Measure blood pressure in both arms at the first visit and use the arm with the higher reading from then on.
