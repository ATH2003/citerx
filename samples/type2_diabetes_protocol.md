# Adult Type 2 Diabetes: Outpatient Management Protocol

Synthetic sample document written for the CiteRx demo. It summarises widely taught
principles for illustration only and is not clinical guidance.

## 1. Scope

This protocol covers adults aged 18 and over with type 2 diabetes managed in primary care.
It does not cover pregnancy, type 1 diabetes, or inpatient hyperglycaemia.

## 2. Diagnosis

Type 2 diabetes is diagnosed with an HbA1c of 6.5% (48 mmol/mol) or higher, a fasting plasma glucose of 7.0 mmol/L or higher, or a 2-hour glucose of 11.1 mmol/L or higher during an oral glucose tolerance test.
In a person without symptoms, a single abnormal result should be confirmed with a repeat test.

## 3. Glycaemic Targets

For most adults the HbA1c target is below 7.0% (53 mmol/mol).
A less strict target of up to 8.0% (64 mmol/mol) is reasonable for older adults with frailty, limited life expectancy, or a history of severe hypoglycaemia.
HbA1c should be measured every 3 months until stable, then every 6 months.

## 4. Pharmacological Therapy

### 4.1 First-Line Therapy: Metformin

Metformin is the preferred first-line medicine unless contraindicated or not tolerated.
Start at 500 mg once daily with the evening meal and increase by 500 mg every 1 to 2 weeks to reduce gastrointestinal side effects.
The usual maximum dose is 2000 mg per day in divided doses.

WARNING: Metformin is contraindicated when eGFR is below 30 mL/min/1.73m2 because of the risk of lactic acidosis.
Do not start metformin when eGFR is between 30 and 45 mL/min/1.73m2; if the patient is already taking it, review the dose and do not exceed 1000 mg per day.

Check renal function before starting metformin and at least once a year afterwards, or every 3 to 6 months when eGFR is below 60.
Long-term metformin use can lower vitamin B12 levels, so check B12 if neuropathy or anaemia develops.

### 4.2 Iodinated Contrast

CAUTION: Withhold metformin at the time of, or before, an iodinated contrast procedure in patients with eGFR between 30 and 60, and re-check eGFR 48 hours afterwards before restarting.

### 4.3 Second-Line Options

If HbA1c remains above target after 3 months of metformin at the maximum tolerated dose, add a second agent.
For people with established cardiovascular disease, heart failure or chronic kidney disease, prefer an SGLT2 inhibitor or a GLP-1 receptor agonist with proven cardiovascular benefit.
Sulfonylureas are a low-cost option but carry a risk of hypoglycaemia and weight gain.

WARNING: SGLT2 inhibitors can cause euglycaemic diabetic ketoacidosis. Stop them 3 days before planned major surgery and during acute illness with reduced oral intake.

## 5. Hypoglycaemia

Hypoglycaemia is a blood glucose below 4.0 mmol/L.
Treat a conscious patient with 15 to 20 g of fast-acting carbohydrate, re-check glucose after 15 minutes, and repeat treatment if glucose is still below 4.0 mmol/L.
If the patient is unconscious, do not give anything by mouth; give intramuscular glucagon 1 mg or intravenous glucose and call emergency services.

## 6. Monitoring and Complications

Check blood pressure at every review; the target for most people with diabetes is below 130/80 mmHg.
Screen for kidney disease every year with eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio.
Arrange retinal screening every 1 to 2 years and a foot examination at least once a year.
Offer a statin to adults aged 40 and over for primary prevention of cardiovascular disease.
